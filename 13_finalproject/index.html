<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"></p> <h3>Final Project</h3> <h4>Lumos, Inertial Wand Interface for Real-Time Light Control</h4> <p><br></p> <div class="video-container"> <video controls muted> <source src="mov2.mp4" type="video/mp4"> Your browser does not support the video tag. </video> </div> <p><br></p> <h4>Motivation</h4> I wanted to bridge my understanding between hardware and software, where one component of a project has the ability to control another object. I noticed that I often would not want to get out from a comfortable position in studying to dim or brighten lights. Therefore, inspired by Hogwarts, I wanted to come up with a magical solution where the lights in my room could be controlled by a hand-gestured wand. A sharp flick of the wand would light up a designated circuit. <p><br></p> <h4>Part 0: Setup</h4> Here is everything you will need to build this project: <ol> <li>2 Microcontrollers (ESP32 or XIAO ESP32 are great)</li> <li>1 ITG/MPU Accelerometer</li> <li>1 Resistor (1k<span>&#8486;</span> is sufficient)</li> <li>1 LED</li> <li>2 Li-ion battery backs with female insertions</li> <li>2 male wire insertions compatible with the Li battery female wires</li> <li>1 breadboard</li> <li>General pin-socket wires (8 for the accelerometer, and more for power)</li> <li>Arduino IDE Software</li> <li>1 ESP32 plug-in cable (to upload the code from the Arduino IDE to the microcontrollers)</li> <li>PrusaSlicer, PrusaSlicer software, and PLA Material</li> </ol> <p><br></p> <h4>Part 1: Wand and Light</h4> You can download the wand and lamp files directly by clicking this button: <p class="margin"> </p> <div class="flexrow"> <a id="btn" href="./week13.zip" download>Download Wand stl and Lamp gcode </a> </div> <p class="margin"> </p> To make the wand from scratch, open up Autodesk Fusion to design the wand. The wand has four main parts: <ol> <li>Main: a cut-out cylinder where all the circuitry would go</li> <li>Handle: a doorknob-looking end to the wand, for fastening</li> <li>Tip: a decorative piece for the other end, to make it look like a wand</li> </ol> <p><br></p> <div class="img-container"> <img src="wand1.png"> </div> <p><br></p> Here are all the parameters of the wand, as well as some photos of the wand. The parameters were based on my measurements so that the circuit would fit within the main cylindrical-shaped container. 'main wand' refers to the overall cylinder-shape that holds the circuit. <p><br></p> <table class="myTable"> <tr><th>Name</th><th>Value (mm)</th> <tr><td>main_wand_diameter</td><td>40</td> <tr><td>main_wand_length</td><td>130</td> <tr><td>handle_diameter</td><td>40</td> <tr><td>handle_length</td><td>33</td> <tr><td>tip_length</td><td>150</td> </table> <p><br></p> I then saved the bodies individaully using 'Save Mesh'. This automatically opens up a 3D printing software called PrusaSlicer for me. If you don't have Prusa, download it for your OS and select the appropriate Prusa model you will be using. Let's move onto the lamp. I opened a new sketch in Autodesk and drew a sketch for the side of the lamp. The dimensions are shown in the image below, but feel free to edit these as you desire. After this, I used the Revolver tool to revolve it and make an uncut 3D dimensional lamp. Finally, I Extruded the slanted rectangle in the sketch to intersect with the 3D dimensional lamp. This cut out an individual part of the lamp. <p><br></p> <div class="img-container"> <img src="lamp1.png"> <img src="lamp2.png"> </div> <p><br></p> Next, I used the Circular Pattern tool to revolve this individual piece around. The piece on the left was my final design after 'stretching' some of the dimensions out from the photos above. After this, I inserted the mesh to PrusaSlicer, selected the same edits (PLA, infill, supports) as with the wand, and downloaded the gcode for printing. <p><br></p> <div class="img-container"> <img src="lamp3.png"> <img src="lamp4.png"> </div> <p><br></p> This is what your two objects should look like when done! <p><br></p> <div class="img-container"> <img src="wand3.png"> </div> <p><br></p> <h4>Part 2: Circuit</h4> Let's now build the two circuits. This is what they look like as finished products: <p><br></p> <div class="img-container"> <img src="circuit1.png"> <img src="circuit2.png"> </div> <p><br></p> To handle the wand circuit, I did the following: <ol> <li>Solder the pins onto the XIAO board.</li> <li>Solder the power and gnd wires of the male wire onto the back of the XIAO board. On the back there are two copper platforms that you'll use as a guide to solder the pins onto.</li> <li>Connect the antenna to the XIAO board (no soldering, it's just a little click-on).</li> <li>Grab the MPU and connect the following pins from the MPU to the XIAO: SDA to D4, SCL to D5, 3V3 to 3V3, and GND to GND.</li> </ol> To handle the light circuit, I did the following: <ol> <li>Solder the pins onto the XIAO board.</li> <li>Solder the power and gnd wires of the male wire onto the back of the XIAO board.</li> <li>Connect the antenna to the XIAO board (no soldering, it's just a little click-on).</li> <li>Start creating the 'light-bulb' circuit on the breadboard by connecting the XIAO's GND to the negative power rail.</li> <li>Set up the resistor and LED so that the LED's smaller leg is in line with GND + Resistor. </li> <li>Connect the long leg of the LED to a wire whose end socket is connected to D0 on the XIAO (in the photo, this is the white wire).</li> </ol> Looks like they're all ready to go! Some important notes: <ol> <li>Really make sure you solder it right by keeping the solder within the outlined copper platforms. Let the solder melt to allow it to touch the platform base. This allows us to keep the wire in place.</li> <li>You can test if the battery is alive and the circuit works using a Multimeter! It serves as a useful debugging tool.</li> </ol> <p><br></p> <h4>Part 3: Code</h4> You'll now want to begin coding.
